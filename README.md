# Towards Greener AI for Privacy Preserving Record Linkage
My thesis investigates the intersection of accuracy, privacy, and energy efficiency in Deep Learning-based Privacy-Preserving Record Linkage (PPRL), a task critical for domains like healthcare, e-commerce, and data management. The work specifically evaluates a method where records from two datasets are encoded using Bloom filters, with differential privacy applied via bit-flipping to protect sensitive information. Feature extraction involves computing similarity metrics on all record pairs, which are then fed into neural networks of varying architectures to optimize matching performance. Energy consumption, power usage, memory occupation, and runtime are measured to capture the computational cost of each configuration. Experiments on datasets intentionally corrupted to simulate real-world data imperfections assess trade-offs between Bloom filter parameters, network complexity, training epochs, and privacy noise. Results show that large Bloom filters with low error rates are more resilient to noise, while moderate network depth and shorter training epochs offer an optimal balance between precision and energy use. Specifically, a two-hidden-layer network trained for five epochs achieves high precision (0.9762) with lower energy consumption (14.52 kJ), highlighting a sweet spot for energy-efficient, privacy-preserving record linkage.
